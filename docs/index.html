How it works, in plain language: for every game line and prop, the program takes every book’s price, strips out the book’s built-in margin, and finds the middle (median) fair price across all the books. When one book’s price is better than that fair price by at least 3%, it shows up here. Edge is how far the book’s price sits from the fair price. EV is the expected profit per $1 staked if the consensus is right. SOLID means a 5%+ edge; THIN means 3–5% — real, but inside the noise. The assumption every play rests on: the consensus is right. If the consensus is wrong, the edge is wrong, and a single prop can miss by a wide margin even when the math is right on average. This never places a bet for you.

Arbitrage — guaranteed profit

What this is: the best price on each side of a market, taken from two different books, adds up to less than 100%. Bet both sides in the stake split shown and the profit is locked in no matter who wins. No model, no prediction — just arithmetic. Arbs are rare on these books and usually under 1%; books may limit accounts that bet them often.

Model props — v5 projection picks

What this is: a separate forward test. A projection model (recency-weighted per-player stats, matchup, home/away) estimates what each player will do, and flags a prop when its projection implies 5%+ expected value against the book's price. These are the model's picks, not the consensus — the backtest lost 0.68% over one week (Oct 19–25, 2026), so treat this as an experiment with a tracked record, not a proven edge. The record is in the banner above and it starts at zero.

Line-move model — forward test

Forward test — NOT a verified winner. This model does not predict games. It watches the line: when the spread moves at least 2 points from the open, money has come in on one side, and the rule follows that side. It comes from the v6 backtest, where the 2-point rule won 57% over two seasons — but that was one pick out of 16 tries, so it still has to prove itself on fresh games. The record below starts at zero and only starts to mean something after 50–100 settled flags. It never places a bet for you.
One honest difference from the backtest: Pinnacle is not in the live odds feed, so the watcher uses the middle (median) move across the books that are there. The open line shown is worked out from that median move.

Manual picks

Called in by hand — NOT the model. These are spread picks and parlays phoned in by friends of the site and added by hand before tipoff. They are graded the same way as the model flags (same morning, same flat $100 stake), but they live in their own record so the line-move model's forward test stays clean. A pick only counts if the line was actually available when it was called — nothing is added after tipoff, unless a timestamped receipt proves it was placed before tipoff.
